\title{Certifying neural eigenpairs and degenerate eigenspaces of the Schr\"odinger
equation: a self-verifying \emph{a posteriori} protocol and its implementation}

\author[inst1]{Chunyang Chen\fnref{fn1}}
\author[inst1]{Yuhao Peng\fnref{fn1}\corref{cor1}}
\ead{pngyuo@cug.edu.cn}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\fntext[fn1]{These authors contributed equally to this work.}
\affiliation[inst1]{organization={School of Mathematics and Physics,

\begin{abstract}
Neural-network solvers for the stationary Schr\"odinger equation are usually
evaluated by comparing with a reference solution that, in genuinely new
problems, is not available. We present a computable \emph{a posteriori}
certificate for neural eigenpairs of the discretized Schr\"odinger operator:
given an approximate eigenfunction it returns a two-sided enclosure of the
corresponding eigenvalue and an upper bound on the angle to the true
eigenfunction, using only quantities computed from the approximation itself.
The hypotheses of the classical Temple and Davis--Kahan bounds---that an
interval contains exactly one eigenvalue---are \emph{verified} rather than
assumed, by counting eigenvalues below a shift with Sturm sequences in one
dimension and with the Sylvester inertia of a sparse LU factorization in two;
the one-dimensional count, and the two-dimensional one for separable
potentials, are re-derived in directed-rounding interval arithmetic, so that
they are theorems rather than well-guarded floating-point estimates.
At a degenerate level no single eigenfunction exists to be certified, so we
extend the construction from states to invariant subspaces: the multiplicity
is \emph{measured} by counting, each eigenvalue of the cluster is bracketed to
within the residual of its Ritz pair, and a principal-angle bound certifies the subspace
that a set of network outputs spans, at no extra hypothesis on their mutual
separation. We describe the implementation, the floating-point safeguards
that make the counting trustworthy, and a protocol that uses the certificate
to detect degeneracy, to select a leakage-free residual for training, and to
choose the truncation order of a spectral filter without knowledge of the
exact solution; a transport lemma carries the certified angle bound back to
the raw network output. Experiments on three one-dimensional and three
two-dimensional potentials, three seeds each, accept $18/18$ enclosures in the
main sweep and $39/39$ over the main sweep plus the twenty-one runs of the
leakage audit, each containing an independently computed discrete eigenvalue;
in two dimensions the reference is a dense LAPACK solve, not the ARPACK values
that propose the intervals. They also give
a quantitative separation of network error from discretization error, correct
refusal of a structurally degenerate state whose residual is
$4\times10^{-12}$ by the single-state certificate, and its certification as a
two-dimensional invariant subspace by the cluster certificate, which separates
the pairs of independently trained networks that span that subspace from
those that collapsed, and prices the ambiguous case as a much weaker bound; a
controlled splitting of the level shows that
the boundary between the two certificates is the counting resolution, not
degeneracy itself---a state is refused at $0.95$ times the resolution and
accepted at $1.01$ times it. Because every such statement concerns the
discretization, we add a variational bound for the continuous operator: the
interpolant of a sampled eigenfunction is a conforming trial function, so its
Rayleigh quotient bounds the continuous eigenvalue from above for every
potential, while a matching lower bound is available for the infinite well
and the box and, we show, fails for the finite well, whose certified discrete energy can lie
\emph{above} the continuous one. The individual bounds are classical; the
contribution is the protocol that makes their hypotheses computable, the
subspace extension that removes the only structural obstacle to using them,
the directed-rounding count that makes the one- and separable two-dimensional
counts theorems, the variational bound that carries one side of the guarantee
to the continuous operator, and an audit of what neural eigenpairs can and
cannot be certified.
\end{abstract}

\begin{programsummary}
\noindent\textbf{Program title:} \texttt{nn-schrodinger-certify}\\
\textbf{CPC Library link to program files:} (to be assigned)\\
\textbf{Developer's repository link:}\\
\url{https://github.com/YuhaoPeng1103/PINN_equation}\\
\textbf{Licensing provisions:} MIT\\
\textbf{Programming language:} Python 3.11 (NumPy, SciPy; PyTorch for
training)\\
\textbf{Nature of problem:} Neural solvers of the stationary Schr\"odinger
equation are evaluated against a reference that a new problem does not have; a
small residual does not bound the eigenfunction error, and at a degenerate
level no single eigenfunction is defined.
\textbf{Solution method:} Given an approximate eigenfunction, or an
$m$-dimensional trial subspace, on a finite-difference grid, the code returns a
two-sided enclosure of the discrete eigenvalue, a bound on the angle to the
exact eigenfunction or invariant subspace, and a verified state index. The
hypotheses of the Kato--Temple and Davis--Kahan bounds are decided by counting
the spectrum with Sturm sequences in one dimension and with the Sylvester
inertia of a no-pivot sparse LU in two, guarded against silent pivoting and
against a resolution floor below which the interval is refused; the
one-dimensional count, and the two-dimensional one for separable potentials,
are re-derived in directed-rounding interval arithmetic and refuse when a
pivot or a summed pair is unresolved. A variational bound evaluated on the
P1/Q1 interpolant of a sampled eigenfunction bounds the continuous eigenvalue
from above without a reference solution, and a certificate-selected Dirichlet
sine filter and a transport inequality extend the bound to the raw network
output.
\textbf{Additional comments:} Includes the drivers that regenerate every table
and figure of the article and a 143-test suite locking the numerical
safeguards. A count costs $0.03$\,s at $81\times81$ and $0.4$\,ms at
$N=2001$, or $5$\,ms in one dimension with directed rounding; a full certificate costs $0.07$\,s at $N=2001$ and $0.2$\,s at
$81\times81$.
\end{programsummary}
